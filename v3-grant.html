How to read this board

What's on this board. A chart-replayed backtest of Grant's single-order Super Mario fib — no L1, one entry. Eight exit outcomes are scored on every trade at once. They are not eight strategies competing on separate samples; they are eight ways of pricing the same set of real trades.

1m and 15s are shown separately, never blended. Pick a timeframe up top; everything below reflects only that timeframe's trades.

The fib is fixed, so every level is forced. Stop 0.085, entry 0.382, TP 0.68. That makes 1R = 0.297 in fib units, and the whole board hangs off four levels: 1R = fib 0.679 (the TP itself), 2R = 0.976, 3R = 1.272, 5R = 1.866.

Family 1 — full position, no partial. Four fixed targets, binary by construction: +NR if Max Run reached it, −1R if not. Full TP @ 1R is a pure 1:1 bet whose expectancy is nothing but 2 × win% − 1. The fifth row, Full size → BE + trail, is the no-partial control: same full size, but no ceiling — it takes whatever structure gives.

Family 2 — 50% off at the 0.68, then the runner. Banking half at the TP locks 0.5R (the TP is 1R away, at half size). The remaining half then carries everything. Because it's half size it must make 2R to move the position 1R — so the runner needs 3R for a 2R trade (fib 1.272) and 5R for a 3R trade (fib 1.866).

That's why the two families share price levels. "Full TP @ 3R" and "runner capped at 1.272" are the same line on the chart — they differ only in how much size is still on when price gets there. Same for 5R and 1.866.

The uncapped trail is the incumbent, kept as a baseline. It's what Grant is running now: half off, then let BOS run with no ceiling. The two capped variants are being tested against it, not against each other.

Everything is one reach curve. All seven numbers are determined by which R levels Max Run cleared — shown directly in the strip under the headline. The targets don't fail independently, so two outcomes agreeing is not two pieces of evidence.

The curve under each row is that outcome's cumulative R, trade by trade, in the order they happened. Green and rising means ahead net; red and sinking means behind.

A green sig tag means the 95% confidence interval clears zero — the edge holds at 95% on its own. No tag means the interval still crosses zero: read it as this log's best lean so far, not a proven result.

Max drawdown is the worst peak-to-trough dip in cumulative R. LIS and WIS count the longest runs of consecutive losing or winning trades, regardless of size.
