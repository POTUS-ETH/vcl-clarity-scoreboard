How to read this board

What's on this board. A chart-replayed backtest of Grant's single-order Super Mario fib — no L1, one entry. Two exit managements are scored on the same trades at once: closing the whole position at TP with no partial, versus banking half at TP and trailing the rest on break-of-structure. They're both hypotheses about the same real trade, not separate strategies to pick between live.

1m and 15s are shown separately, never blended. Pick a timeframe up top; everything below reflects only that timeframe's trades.

The fib is fixed, so the payoff is too. Stop sits at 0.085, entry at 0.382, TP at 0.68. That spacing puts the TP almost exactly 1R from entry by construction — reward 0.68−0.382 = 0.298 against risk 0.382−0.085 = 0.297. It isn't a property of any one trade; it falls out of the layout.

Full @ TP — no partial. Close the entire position the instant price hits TP. Because of the geometry above this is a 1:1 bet — always +1R or −1R, never anything else, so its expectancy is nothing more than its win rate: 2 × win% − 1. It breaks even at exactly 50% and has no upside tail at all.

50% @ TP → trail BOS. Bank half at TP, move the rest to breakeven, trail the remainder on break-of-structure. Scored at wherever the trail actually exited, so it's part realized result, part hypothesis. −1 if TP never clears.

So the whole comparison reduces to one question: does the runner beat 1R? On a winner this management returns 0.5 + 0.5 × (whatever the trail got). It beats Full @ TP only when the trail exceeds 1R, and it costs half an R whenever the runner retraces to breakeven. Every bit of the gap between the two rows is the runner — nothing else can move it.

The curve under each row is that management's cumulative R, trade by trade, in the order the trades actually happened — not dollars, just R stacked on itself. Green and rising means it's ahead net; red and sinking means it's behind.

Reading a dot-and-line mark (the ranked table below). Dot = mean R (expectancy). Line = 95% confidence interval. A green "sig" tag means that interval clears zero — the edge is real at 95% confidence. No tag means the line still crosses zero: read the number as this log's best lean so far, not a proven result.

Max drawdown, LIS, and WIS are three different things. Max drawdown is the worst peak-to-trough dip in cumulative R — a big win followed by a few small losses can still be a shallow drawdown. LIS (losses in succession) and WIS (wins in succession) count the longest run of consecutive losing or winning trades, regardless of size.

The head-to-head margin is paired. Both managements are computed off the same Max Run on the same trades, so the delta between them is a trade-by-trade paired comparison, not two independent samples.
